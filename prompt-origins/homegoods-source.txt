10-SECOND DAILY VLOG MODE
When the requested video duration is 10 seconds, prioritize a fast-paced real-life daily-vlog montage rather than a traditional product commercial.
The video should feel like:
“Someone casually filmed this useful product during a normal moment at home.”
NOT:
“A 3D product animation with multiple camera angles.”
CORE STYLE
Use approximately 5–8 short shots within 10 seconds.
Each shot should last roughly 0.7–2 seconds, depending on the action.
The camera should continuously feel human-operated, as if the person filming is physically moving around the room and capturing different moments with a smartphone.
The viewer should feel like they are watching fragments of a real daily routine.
10-SECOND RHYTHM
Use this general structure:
0:00–0:01.2 — HOOK
Start with an interesting detail, problem, action, or visually satisfying moment.
0:01.2–0:02.4 — CONTEXT
Quickly reveal the environment or situation.
0:02.4–0:03.6 — PRODUCT
Reveal the product from another angle.
0:03.6–0:05.0 — ACTION
Show the product performing its useful function.
0:05.0–0:06.3 — DETAIL
Cut to a close-up or macro detail.
0:06.3–0:08.0 — RESULT
Show the visible transformation or benefit.
0:08.0–0:10.0 — PAYOFF
Show the finished result with a natural handheld camera move.
This is a flexible framework, not a mandatory sequence.
Adapt the timing to the product.
MULTI-ANGLE DAILY VLOG LANGUAGE
Use frequent but motivated changes in:
Camera distance
Camera height
Camera angle
Direction
Framing
Shot size
Camera position
Possible sequence:
Wide → Medium → Close-up → Macro → Side angle → Top-down → Wide/Medium reveal
Do NOT make every shot look perfectly composed.
Allow slight natural differences between shots.
This should feel like a person quickly capturing interesting moments, not a professional camera rig following a programmed shot list.
TRANSITIONS
Prioritize natural physical transitions over digital effects.
Good transitions:
Camera moves past an object and cuts.
Product fills the frame and cuts to detail.
Quick handheld reframing.
Movement in one shot continues into the next.
Match movement between two shots.
Cut on action.
Object briefly covers the lens.
Natural whip-pan caused by the person turning the phone.
Camera approaches a surface and cuts to a close-up.
Avoid:
Flash transitions.
Glitch transitions.
3D transitions.
Spin transitions.
Artificial light streaks.
Overly cinematic transitions.
Random effects.
The cuts should feel like real vlog editing.
HUMAN-OPERATED CAMERA CONTINUITY
Although the video contains no visible person, every shot should imply that the same invisible person is filming.
For example:
Shot 1:
The camera is standing several steps away.
Shot 2:
The person has physically moved closer.
Shot 3:
The person has moved to the side.
Shot 4:
The person lowers the phone.
Shot 5:
The person brings the phone closer for a detail.
Shot 6:
The person steps back to show the result.
This creates the feeling of a real continuous filming session.
Do not make the camera teleport between positions.
CAMERA IMPERFECTION
Allow subtle realistic imperfections:
Slight handheld shake.
Tiny framing corrections.
Small exposure adjustment.
Slight autofocus change.
Natural motion blur.
Small variations in camera height.
Slightly imperfect composition.
These imperfections should be subtle.
Do NOT make the footage shaky or amateurish.
The goal is:
REALISTIC SMARTPHONE FOOTAGE, NOT BAD CAMERA WORK.
PRODUCT PRESERVATION
Even with rapid cuts, the product must remain identical across every shot.
Maintain:
Same shape
Same color
Same material
Same proportions
Same details
Same logo
Same design
No morphing between angles.
EDITING FEEL
The final 10 seconds should feel:
Fast
Casual
Natural
Addictive
Visually varied
Real-life
Smartphone-shot
Lifestyle-oriented
Avoid the feeling of a traditional polished TV advertisement.
Think:
“A person casually filming a useful thing they use during their day.”
rather than:
“A company presenting a product.”
IMPORTANT
Do not increase the number of shots just for the sake of speed.
Every cut must reveal something new:
new angle, new detail, new action, new context, or new result.
The viewer should feel that something new is happening every 1–2 seconds.
Final priority:
REAL DAILY VLOG FEEL > CINEMATIC COMMERCIAL FEEL
MULTIPLE REAL CAMERA ANGLES > AUTOMATED CAMERA MOVEMENT
FAST VISUAL STORYTELLING > LONG PRODUCT SHOTS
NATURAL CUTS > SPECIAL EFFECT TRANSITIONS

HANDHELD PRODUCT REVIEW MODE
When the requested video uses HANDHELD PRODUCT REVIEW MODE, create a realistic short-form product review where an invisible person uses their hand to hold and physically inspect the product, especially its outer shell, body, surface, material, construction, size, and finish.
The video should feel like:
“Someone is casually holding this product and showing me what the outside looks like.”
NOT:
“A cinematic product commercial with complicated camera movements.”

1. SIMPLE REVIEW STYLE
Use approximately 3–5 shots within 10 seconds.
Keep the editing relatively simple.
Do NOT use rapid 8–10 shot editing.
Do NOT constantly change camera angles.
Do NOT add unnecessary actions.
The main appeal comes from:
REAL HAND + REAL PRODUCT + REAL PHYSICAL MOVEMENT + CLEAR VISUAL INSPECTION

2. HUMAN HAND MUST CAUSE EVERY PRODUCT MOVEMENT
Whenever the product moves, the visible hand must clearly cause the movement.
Examples:
hand grips product → lifts it
hand gently shakes product → product physically moves
hand tilts product → product tilts
hand rotates product → product rotates
hand squeezes or touches surface → product responds naturally
Never allow the product to move by itself.
The viewer must clearly understand the physical cause and effect.

3. OUTER-SHELL REVIEW ACTION
The main action should be a natural hand-held inspection of the product exterior.
Depending on the actual product, the hand may:
gently lift it
gently shake it
tilt it left and right
rotate it slightly
turn it to show the side
bring it slightly closer to the camera
run fingers naturally over the exterior surface
lightly tap the exterior when appropriate
show the edges, seams, surface, or finish
Use ONLY actions that make sense for the actual product.
Do NOT invent claims.
For example:
Do not shake a product and automatically claim it is “super durable” unless that durability is actually supported.
The video should visually show the product's construction rather than inventing specifications.

4. NATURAL “LẮC LẮC” MOVEMENT
The shaking motion should look like a real person casually checking the product.
Use:
small wrist movement
gentle back-and-forth motion
realistic hand grip
natural inertia
subtle product weight
realistic movement of the wrist and fingers
The shaking should NOT be:
violent
exaggerated
perfectly repetitive
robotic
perfectly symmetrical
mechanically timed
The hand should naturally vary the movement slightly.
Example:
lift → gentle shake left/right → stop → tilt slightly → show exterior

5. REAL HAND APPEARANCE
The hand must look genuinely photographed by a smartphone.
Preserve realistic:
skin texture
pores
wrinkles
knuckles
fingernails
finger proportions
joints
wrist anatomy
natural grip pressure
Avoid:
extra fingers
fused fingers
malformed fingers
plastic skin
floating hands
impossible joints
distorted wrists
unnatural finger positions
The hand must physically contact the product correctly.

6. REAL-LIFE BACKGROUND
The background must look like a real physical location, not an AI-generated set.
Use a believable everyday environment appropriate to the product:
home
kitchen
bedroom
desk
living room
student room
bathroom
dining area
workspace
Keep realistic:
walls
floor
furniture
everyday objects
natural clutter
small imperfections
realistic materials
natural shadows
real reflections
believable perspective
The background should feel ordinary and lived-in.
Avoid:
luxury showroom
commercial studio
perfect minimalist room
fake decorative set
futuristic interior
excessive decoration
perfectly symmetrical composition
CGI-looking environment

7. BACKGROUND MUST NEVER CHANGE
Keep the EXACT SAME physical environment throughout the entire video.
Do NOT change:
room
wall
floor
furniture
table
shelf
window
surrounding objects
object positions
lighting
shadows
reflections
The camera may move slightly, but the physical environment remains the same.

8. CAMERA STYLE
Use a realistic smartphone camera.
The camera should remain relatively stable compared with the fast daily-vlog mode.
Use:
subtle handheld movement
tiny natural framing adjustments
realistic autofocus
natural motion blur
slight exposure adjustment
realistic smartphone perspective
Avoid:
floating camera
robotic movement
drone movement
perfect orbit
complicated camera paths
automatic tracking
excessive camera movement
cinematic commercial camera moves
The main movement should come from the hand and product, not from the camera.

9. RECOMMENDED 10-SECOND STRUCTURE
0.0–2.0s — HOOK
Start with the product already in the hand or with the hand naturally grabbing it.
Immediately show the exterior.
Possible:
close-up of surface
hand lifting product
hand gently shaking product
unusual exterior detail
textured material
The viewer should immediately understand:
“This video is showing me what this product actually looks like in hand.”

2.0–4.5s — EXTERIOR INSPECTION
Keep the same general camera position.
The hand gently moves the product:
tilt → slight shake → turn slightly
Show:
front
side
outer shell
surface
finish
Do not perform unrelated actions.

4.5–6.5s — CLOSE DETAIL
Use one simple cut to a closer view.
Show an important exterior detail:
texture
seam
edge
handle
surface finish
button
opening
construction detail
The hand remains naturally connected to the product.

6.5–8.2s — SECOND ANGLE
One more simple angle.
The hand gently rotates or tilts the same product to reveal another side.
No unnecessary movement.

8.2–10.0s — FINAL IMPRESSION
Show the product naturally held in frame.
Use a subtle:
slight shake → stop → hold steady
or
tilt → return to normal → hold
End on the product's most visually convincing exterior detail.

10. FEW-CUT RULE
Keep editing simple.
Target:
3–5 shots / 10 seconds
Do NOT increase the shot count just to create visual variety.
The viewer should have time to actually inspect the product.
Preferred rhythm:
HOOK → INSPECT → DETAIL → SECOND ANGLE → FINAL HOLD

11. NO UNNECESSARY ACTION
Do NOT add:
random opening and closing
repeated shaking
unnecessary rotation
random tapping
random button pressing
placing product down and picking it up again
unnecessary camera movement
unrelated objects entering frame
exaggerated hand gestures
The product should be doing ONE clear thing:
being naturally held and visually inspected.

12. PRODUCT LOCK
The product must remain exactly identical to the reference.
Preserve:
shape
proportions
dimensions
color
material
texture
exterior design
logo
text
buttons
handles
seams
edges
all visible details
No:
redesign
morphing
replacement
deformation
changed proportions
changed color
changed material
missing details
added details
duplicated product

13. REALISTIC PHYSICS
The product must have realistic:
weight
inertia
friction
grip
contact
rotation
movement
When the hand shakes the product, the product must respond with believable physical inertia.
When the hand stops, the product should naturally stop.
Nothing should float or move independently.

14. RETENTION
This mode uses fewer cuts, so retention must come from physical inspection and curiosity, not rapid editing.
Create a simple visual progression:
WHAT IS IT? → HOW DOES THE OUTSIDE LOOK? → WHAT DOES THE MATERIAL/SURFACE LOOK LIKE? → WHAT IS THE FINISH LIKE? → FINAL VIEW
The viewer should continuously discover a new visual detail without requiring constant cuts.

15. FINAL STYLE
The final video should feel:
casual
realistic
trustworthy
simple
smartphone-shot
human-operated
product-focused
natural
believable
Think:
“Someone picked up the product and casually showed me what it looks and feels like in real life.”
NOT:
“A brand produced a cinematic product advertisement.”
