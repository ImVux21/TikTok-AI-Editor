10-SECOND DAILY VLOG MODE
When the requested video duration is 10 seconds, prioritize a fast-paced real-life daily-vlog montage rather than a traditional product commercial.
The video should feel like:
“Someone casually filmed this useful product during a normal moment at home.”
NOT:
“A 3D product animation with multiple camera angles.”
CORE STYLE
Use approximately 5–8 short shots within 10 seconds.
Each shot should last roughly 0.7–2 seconds, depending on the action.
The camera should continuously feel human-operated, as if the person filming is physically moving around the room and capturing different moments with a smartphone.
The viewer should feel like they are watching fragments of a real daily routine.
10-SECOND RHYTHM
Use this general structure:
0:00–0:01.2 — HOOK
Start with an interesting detail, problem, action, or visually satisfying moment.
0:01.2–0:02.4 — CONTEXT
Quickly reveal the environment or situation.
0:02.4–0:03.6 — PRODUCT
Reveal the product from another angle.
0:03.6–0:05.0 — ACTION
Show the product performing its useful function.
0:05.0–0:06.3 — DETAIL
Cut to a close-up or macro detail.
0:06.3–0:08.0 — RESULT
Show the visible transformation or benefit.
0:08.0–0:10.0 — PAYOFF
Show the finished result with a natural handheld camera move.
This is a flexible framework, not a mandatory sequence.
Adapt the timing to the product.
MULTI-ANGLE DAILY VLOG LANGUAGE
Use frequent but motivated changes in:
Camera distance
Camera height
Camera angle
Direction
Framing
Shot size
Camera position
Possible sequence:
Wide → Medium → Close-up → Macro → Side angle → Top-down → Wide/Medium reveal
Do NOT make every shot look perfectly composed.
Allow slight natural differences between shots.
This should feel like a person quickly capturing interesting moments, not a professional camera rig following a programmed shot list.
TRANSITIONS
Prioritize natural physical transitions over digital effects.
Good transitions:
Camera moves past an object and cuts.
Product fills the frame and cuts to detail.
Quick handheld reframing.
Movement in one shot continues into the next.
Match movement between two shots.
Cut on action.
Object briefly covers the lens.
Natural whip-pan caused by the person turning the phone.
Camera approaches a surface and cuts to a close-up.
Avoid:
Flash transitions.
Glitch transitions.
3D transitions.
Spin transitions.
Artificial light streaks.
Overly cinematic transitions.
Random effects.
The cuts should feel like real vlog editing.
HUMAN-OPERATED CAMERA CONTINUITY
Although the video contains no visible person, every shot should imply that the same invisible person is filming.
For example:
Shot 1:
The camera is standing several steps away.
Shot 2:

Shot 4:

Shot 6:

This creates the feeling of a real continuous filming session.
Do not make the camera teleport between positions.
CAMERA IMPERFECTION
Allow subtle realistic imperfections:
Slight handheld shake.
Tiny framing corrections.
Small exposure adjustment.
Slight autofocus change.
Natural motion blur.
Small variations in camera height.
Slightly imperfect composition.
These imperfections should be subtle.
Do NOT make the footage shaky or amateurish.
The goal is:
REALISTIC SMARTPHONE FOOTAGE, NOT BAD CAMERA WORK.
